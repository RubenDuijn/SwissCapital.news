## 2) Principal findings

1. AI commercial momentum is visible, but conversion quality is uneven.
- Microsoft discloses strong cloud and AI-linked growth metrics and very high investment intensity in property and equipment additions.
- Interpretation: revenue growth can coexist with large capital absorption, so free cash flow quality must be evaluated explicitly.

2. Capex cycles are now directly rate-sensitive through discounting and financing channels.
- Fed and SNB materials both indicate inflation-risk awareness and policy uncertainty.
- Interpretation: valuation outcomes are sensitive to real-rate persistence even if top-line AI growth remains robust.

3. Energy is a binding second-order variable for AI economics.
- IEA and EIA publications both support elevated relevance of electricity demand growth and power-price volatility.
- Interpretation: data-center economics and inference cost curves should not be modeled independently of power systems.

4. Macro tailwinds and fragilities coexist.
- World Bank and BIS sources note AI-linked investment support but also elevated geopolitical and fiscal risk channels.

## 3) Competing interpretations

Interpretation A (bull):
- AI investment transitions into broad productivity gains and earnings breadth beyond infrastructure leaders.

Interpretation B (base):
- Value creation is substantial but concentrated; returns diverge by capital efficiency, distribution strength, and execution quality.

Interpretation C (bear):
- Capex outpaces commercialization; depreciation, competition, and financing costs compress returns and valuations.

## 4) Proposed article structure

1. Opening thesis: AI has entered an infrastructure economics phase.
2. What is measured today: capex, cloud growth, and operational conversion indicators.
3. Mechanism distinction: revenue growth vs cost savings vs productivity vs speculative repricing.
4. Macro overlay: inflation/rates and valuation sensitivity.
5. Labor/productivity margin channel over 1-5 years.
6. Scenario table (bull/base/bear) with assumptions and trigger indicators.
7. Conclusion: what to monitor next for earnings breadth and valuation durability.

## 5) Risks of analytical overreach

- Treating management guidance as realized economics.
- Extrapolating hyperscaler economics to all adopters.
- Ignoring depreciation and replacement-cycle drag.
- Using energy demand narratives without a dated power-price context.
