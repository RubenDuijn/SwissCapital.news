## 2) Principal findings

1. Energy-price risk remains a live macro channel.
- EIA explicitly embeds constrained Middle East flows assumptions and revised Brent forecast path in October 2026.
- Transmission relevance: inflation expectations, transport/input costs, and policy-rate path sensitivity.

2. Central banks still emphasize inflation persistence and uncertainty.
- Fed SEP and SNB communication both retain uncertainty framing and inflation vigilance, even with projected moderation.
- Transmission relevance: discount-rate pressure and valuation sensitivity for long-duration equities.

3. Geopolitical friction increasingly acts through policy architecture.
- Section 301 tariff architecture remains in force and creates cost/compliance friction.
- Export-control governance remains an active strategic technology channel.

4. Growth resilience and fragility coexist.
- World Bank and BIS materials point to resilience from AI investment and demand support, but downside risk from conflict/energy/fiscal strain.

## 3) Competing interpretations

Interpretation A (constructive):
- Markets have partially absorbed conflict risk; absent major physical disruption, growth slows but does not break.

Interpretation B (cautionary):
- Current pricing underestimates second-round inflation and financing-cost persistence if energy shocks recur.

Interpretation C (regime-shift):
- Trade and technology fragmentation creates durable cost inefficiency and structurally higher risk premia.

## 4) Proposed article structure

1. Opening thesis: Events matter when transmission channels bind.
2. Energy and shipping channel: what is currently measurable versus speculative.
3. US-China policy channel: tariffs, controls, and supply-chain cost architecture.
4. Europe channel: energy dependence, growth asymmetry, and policy constraints.
5. Market channel: inflation/rates -> multiples/credit/risk appetite.
6. Scenario table for 6-18 months with confirmation/invalidation indicators.
7. Conclusion: what to monitor next and where markets may already discount risk.

## 5) Risks of analytical overreach

- Over-attributing every geopolitical headline to durable market repricing.
- Treating model forecasts as observed data.
- Ignoring policy-response elasticity (SPR, convoy routing, substitution effects).
- Ignoring valuation starting points when projecting downside.
