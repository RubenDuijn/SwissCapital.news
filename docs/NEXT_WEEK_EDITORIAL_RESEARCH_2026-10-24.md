## D) Detailed Dossier and Outline for First Article

### Working headline
The AI Infrastructure Bet: Where Returns Will Concentrate and Where Capital Will Be Trapped

### Subtitle
A value-chain map of AI economics across chips, cloud, power, data centers, software, and enterprise adopters.

### Central thesis
AI demand is real, but returns will not be evenly distributed. Economic profits should concentrate where capacity is scarce, switching costs are high, and utilization can stay elevated; commoditized or balance-sheet-heavy layers without pricing power face return compression.

### Key question
Which AI value-chain segments can convert current capex intensity into durable free-cash-flow returns after financing and energy constraints?

### Intended audience and value
- Institutional and advanced private investors, strategy teams, and policy-aware executives.
- Value: a testable framework for separating revenue growth from investable return quality.

### Core argument structure

1. Reframe: from AI excitement to return conversion
- Distinguish demand, accounting earnings, free cash flow, and economic profit.

2. Segment-by-segment return map
- Semiconductors: scarcity rents, product cycles, pricing power risks.
- Hyperscalers/cloud: utilization, contract structure, depreciation burden.
- Data-center operators: financing, power access, occupancy, lease pass-through.
- Energy/grid: bottleneck economics, regulated vs merchant return profile.
- Software/app layer: differentiation vs model commoditization.
- Enterprise adopters: productivity realization lag vs spend.

3. Capital discipline lens
- ROIC direction vs WACC under current rate/spread regime.
- Capex-to-operating-cash-flow ratios and payback windows.

4. Rate and energy sensitivity tests
- What changes if real yields remain elevated.
- What changes if energy volatility persists.

5. Counterarguments and falsification tests
- Cases where broad diffusion can still expand aggregate margins.
- Conditions that would invalidate concentration thesis.

6. Portfolio implications (non-advisory)
- Preference for cash-flow-visible, utilization-proven, contract-anchored models.
- Caution on narratives requiring cheap capital and perfect execution.

### Evidence block to include
- Microsoft FY26 Q4 and FY26 annual disclosures.
- NVIDIA Q2 FY27 disclosures and guidance.
- Rate backdrop: DGS10, DFII10, T10YIE.
- Credit backdrop: BAMLH0A0HYM2.
- Energy backdrop: DCOILBRENTEU + EIA STEO + EIA weekly.

### Counterarguments to address explicitly
- Scale economics could broaden faster than expected via model-cost declines.
- Enterprise adoption may move from pilots to large workflow replacement faster than priced.
- Hardware and cloud competition could still enlarge TAM enough to protect aggregate returns.

### What would weaken or invalidate thesis
- Sustained margin expansion among broad downstream adopters without proportional capex escalation.
- Durable decline in real yields and energy volatility reducing financing and operating frictions.
- Evidence of stable pricing power across currently commoditization-prone layers.

### Overlap control with existing SwissCapital articles
- Existing AI article: macro conversion thesis.
- New article: explicit value-chain attribution and return-math framework.
- Existing geopolitics article: transmission channels.
- New article: microeconomics of return capture by segment.

### Proposed conclusion direction
AI is not one trade. It is a multi-layer capital cycle where durable returns likely cluster in bottlenecked and operationally advantaged layers, while capital-heavy commoditizing layers face lower realized economic returns than top-line growth implies.
